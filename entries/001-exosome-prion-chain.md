## Evidence tiers

Four different claims get substituted for one another in this literature. Keeping them apart is what makes the drift visible.

- **T1** The protein is **present in** a vesicle preparation.
- **T2** It **transfers between cultured cells**, with a readout in the recipient.
- **T3** It **seeds aggregation in an animal** after inoculation.
- **T4** It **transmits clinical disease to an animal**, with an attack rate.

The claim above is a **T4** claim. Février 2004 and Vella 2007 both report **T4** results (intracerebral bioassays, different groups and strains); the other readable papers report **T1 or T2** work.

## The chain

Verification reflects checks on 6 October 2026: all eight nodes confirmed against the publishers' own pages, full text read for each.

### 2004 — Origin — an exosome bioassay in mice
Tier: T4
Verification: Verified at source
Tags: Peer-reviewed; Independently corroborated

Février B, Vilette D, Archer F, Loew D, Faigle W, Vidal M, Laude H, Raposo G. "Cells release prions in association with exosomes." *Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A.* 2004;101(26):9683–9688. [doi:10.1073/pnas.0308413101](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0308413101)

> "we found that exosomes bearing PrPsc are infectious."

- **What it shows:** An intracerebral bioassay (Table 1): the 100,000 × g exosome pellet from scrapie-infected Rov cells — and that fraction purified on a sucrose gradient — injected into tg338 mice (overexpress ovine PrP), strain 127. Pellet 5/5, 76.8 ± 2.6 days; purified fraction 5/5, 78 ± 1.2 days; diluent control 0/5, >200 days.
- **What it does not show:** Transmission by any route other than injection into the brain, or in mice with normal PrP levels.
- **Note:** Independent of Vella 2007 (different group, different strain) and concordant with it — two separate demonstrations of the same claim.
- **Files:** Full text read 6 Oct 2026.

### 2007 — The in-vivo experiment — read at source
Tier: T4
Verification: Verified at source
Tags: Peer-reviewed; Independently corroborated; Load-bearing

Vella LJ, Sharples RA, Lawson VA, Masters CL, Cappai R, Hill AF. "Packaging of prions into exosomes is associated with a novel pathway of PrP processing." *Journal of Pathology* 2007;211(5):582–590. [doi:10.1002/path.2145](https://doi.org/10.1002/path.2145)

> "these exosomes produced prion disease when inoculated into mice."

- **What it shows:** An intracerebral bioassay (Table 1): material from M1000-infected moRK13 and GT1-7 cells injected into Tga20 mice, which overexpress mouse PrP. Infected exosomes — 5/5 mice developed disease, mean incubation 90–98 days. Non-infected controls — 0/3 and 0/4, no disease past 300–370 days.
- **What it does not show:** Transmission by any route other than injection into the brain, or in mice with normal PrP levels; and how the exosomes' infectivity compares with brain tissue (no titration). Concordant with Février 2004 — an independent demonstration, different group and strain.
- **Cites:** Février 2004 and others, for the vesicle association.
- **Files:** Full text read 6 Oct 2026.

### 2008 — A third reference, often bundled with the first two
Tier: T2
Verification: Verified at source

## What the trace found

Traced expecting citation drift. On reading the sources, the core claim largely holds — two independent primary studies support it. The residual issues are narrower than the downstream language suggests.

1. **The claim is supported.** Février 2004 and Vella 2007 — different groups, different prion strains — each show exosome-associated prions cause disease when injected into the brains of PrP-overexpressing mice. The chain is not built on an unverifiable source.
2. **But the scope is narrow.** Both use the most permissive assay: intracerebral injection into mice that overexpress PrP. Downstream text repeats "transmit to animals" without that scope, so the claim reads broader than what was shown.
3. **One citation points to the wrong paper.** Hartmann 2017 cites Guo 2016 — a cell-culture-only study — for the in-vivo claim. The claim is true, but Guo is not where it was shown; the correct sources are Février and Vella. (Hartmann also cites Vella correctly elsewhere.)
4. **Tiers get bundled.** The 2012 review merges references spanning cell-culture and animal work into one phrase ("animal and cell bioassays"), so a reader cannot tell which reference supports which tier.

## Where it stands

- **Established.** Two independent studies — Février 2004 (sheep strain) and Vella 2007 (human-derived strain), different groups — show exosome-associated prions cause disease when injected into the brains of PrP-overexpressing mice (both 5/5; ~77 and ~90–98 days; clean controls).
- **Still open.** Both demonstrations use the most permissive assay. These are the tests that would extend the claim beyond it.
    - transmission by any route other than injection into the brain
    - transmission in mice with normal PrP levels
    - how infectious the exosomes are compared with brain tissue
