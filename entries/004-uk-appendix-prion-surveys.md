## Evidence tiers

What T1–T4 mean for this claim.

- **T1** Abnormal prion protein is **present** in appendix tissue (immunostaining).
- **T2** That protein **seeds like vCJD** in vitro — amplification plus strain typing against a substrate panel.
- **T3** The original tissue is **infectious** — a bioassay transmits disease.
- **T4** A survey donor **developed, or would have developed, vCJD**.

The claim is a **T3/T4** claim. The evidence reaches **T1** (presence). The T2 test on the survey positives has not been reported; the T3 test on the fixed survey positives showed no transmission (see below); T4 cannot be reached because the tissue was irreversibly unlinked from the donors.

## What the surveys found

Three UK surveys stained archived appendix tissue for abnormal prion protein. The largest (Gill 2013) states: "Of the 32 441 appendix samples 16 were positive for abnormal PrP, indicating an overall prevalence of 493 per million population (95% confidence interval 282 to 801 per million)" — about 1 in 2,000. The positives spanned all three codon-129 genotypes (MM, MV, VV), not only the 129MM genotype of every confirmed clinical vCJD case.

### 2013 — The prevalence survey

Gill ON, Spencer Y, Richard-Loendt A, et al. "Prevalent abnormal prion protein in human appendixes after bovine spongiform encephalopathy epizootic: large scale survey." *BMJ* 2013;347:f5675. [doi:10.1136/bmj.f5675](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.f5675) · PMC3805509 · open access

> "Of the 32 441 appendix samples 16 were positive for abnormal PrP, indicating an overall prevalence of 493 per million population (95% confidence interval 282 to 801 per million)."

- **What it shows:** Abnormal prion protein is present in appendix tissue at about 1 in 2,000. This is the source of the widely quoted figure.
- **What it does not show:** Whether that protein is infectious, is vCJD, or would ever cause disease. Immunostaining detects presence, not any of those.

### 2011 — The direct infectivity test on the survey positives
Tier: T3
Verification: Verified at source
Tags: Peer-reviewed

Wadsworth JDF, Dalmau-Mena I, Joiner S, et al. "Effect of fixation on brain and lymphoreticular vCJD prions and bioassay of key positive specimens from a retrospective vCJD prevalence study." *Journal of Pathology* 2011;223(4):511–518. [doi:10.1002/path.2810](https://doi.org/10.1002/path.2810) · OnlineOpen

> "The absence of detectable infectivity in fixed, known positive vCJD lymphoreticular tissue precludes interpreting negative transmissions from vCJD prevalence study appendix specimens."

- **What it shows:** Two of the three prevalence-study abnormal-PrP-positive appendix specimens were inoculated into transgenic mice overexpressing human PrP (129MM Tg45). Frozen vCJD brain and appendix and formalin-fixed vCJD brain all transmitted infection; formalin-fixed vCJD spleen and both formalin-fixed prevalence-study appendix specimens transmitted nothing (0 of 4 and 0 of 8 mice). vCJD strain properties were preserved and were not altered by fixation.
- **What it does not show:** Whether the survey-positive appendixes are truly non-infectious. The known-positive control — formalin-fixed vCJD spleen — also failed to transmit, so infectivity in fixed lymphoreticular tissue was below the assay's detection limit; the authors state the prevalence-study positives could still be prion-infected at a titre or fixation state their bioassay could not detect. No frozen survey material exists, so the test cannot be repeated on the original specimens.
- **Files:** Full text read at source (publisher PDF supplied) 12 Oct 2026. Quote present verbatim.

### 2021 — Amplification depends on seed and substrate
Tier: T2
Verification: Verified at source
Tags: Peer-reviewed

Bélondrade M, Nicot S, Mayran C, et al. "Sensitive protein misfolding cyclic amplification of sporadic Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease prions is strongly seed and substrate dependent." *Scientific Reports* 2021;11:4058. [doi:10.1038/s41598-021-83604-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-83604-3) · PMC7893054 · open access

> "the amplification obtained in the TgMet substrate in our study was somewhat disappointing, especially in the homologous PMCA context for MM1 and MM2 prions."

- **What it shows:** Amplification of human prions is "strongly seed and substrate dependent." A 129-methionine substrate missed or barely amplified several non-vCJD human prion types. Because the appendix positives are not all 129MM, an amplification assay built on a 129MM substrate could return a negative on a specimen that genuinely seeds but is not vCJD.
- **What it does not show:** Any result on the appendix specimens themselves; this is the methodological constraint, not a test of them.
- **Files:** Full text read at PMC 9 Oct 2026.

### 2022 — A DNA-methylation test of the positives
Tier: T1
Verification: Verified at source
Tags: Peer-reviewed

Guntoro F, Viré E, Giordani C, et al. "DNA methylation analysis of archival lymphoreticular tissues in Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease." *Acta Neuropathologica* 2022;144:785–787. [doi:10.1007/s00401-022-02476-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00401-022-02476-7) · PMC9468073 · open access

> "14/15 test PrP-positive samples were classified as controls (1 as vCJD)."

- **What it shows:** A DNA-methylation classifier trained on confirmed prion disease and tested blind on survey-positive appendix samples classified 14 of 15 as controls rather than as prion disease.
- **What it does not show:** That the positives are false. The authors state that to read this as a false-positive finding "would be an over-interpretation," because "subclinical or preclinical infection may associate with poorly developed DNA me[thylation]" signatures. It is evidence that leans away from established infection, not a disproof.
- **Files:** Full text read at PMC 9 Oct 2026.

### 2023 — The amplification method, demonstrated on a known vCJD case
Tier: T2
Verification: Verified at source
Tags: Peer-reviewed

Suleiman S, McGuire L, Chong A, et al. "Conservation of vCJD strain properties after extraction and in vitro propagation of PrPSc from archived formalin-fixed brain and appendix tissues using highly sensitive protein misfolding cyclic amplification." *Molecular Neurobiology* 2023;60(11):6275–6293. [doi:10.1007/s12035-023-03444-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12035-023-03444-2) · open access

> "In particular, our upcoming research will be dedicated to employing this protocol in extracting PrP aggregates from the positive Appendix study samples, using the extracted material to seed hsPMCA reaction and producing material for animal inoculation."

- **What it shows:** Abnormal prion protein was extracted from formalin-fixed brain and appendix tissue of a single pathologically confirmed vCJD patient, amplified by highly sensitive PMCA, and the amplified product transmitted to two mouse lines (gene-targeted HuMM and wild-type RIII) with the biochemical and strain features of vCJD. The method is proposed as the platform to test the survey positives.
- **What it does not show:** Any result on the Appendix II/III survey positives. Those are named as the target of the authors' "upcoming research"; no such result is reported here. The paper does not report the assay's false-positive rate on known-negative survey-era appendix.
- **Files:** Full text read at source (publisher OA PDF supplied) 12 Oct 2026. Quote present verbatim.

## Where it stands

- **Established.** Abnormal prion protein is present in UK appendix tissue at about 1 in 2,000 (Gill 2013, verified).
- **Unresolved.** Whether it means incubating vCJD. One molecular test (Guntoro 2022) leans against established infection; the direct infectivity test (Wadsworth 2011) found no transmission from the formalin-fixed survey positives, which fixation or a low prion titre could explain; the amplification-plus-strain-typing platform that could discriminate (Suleiman 2023) has been built but not yet run on the positive specimens.
- **Unanswerable.** Whether a given donor would have developed disease. The tissue was irreversibly unlinked from the donors, so no one can be followed up; most pre-BSE donors have since died of other causes without prion disease.

## What would settle it

- Run the amplification assay on the survey positives against a substrate panel (129MM, 129VV, bank vole) and strain-type the product. This distinguishes "vCJD" from "a different prion-like entity" and from "nothing" — which a single-substrate yes/no does not.
- Measure the assay's false-positive rate on known-negative archived appendix of the same processing era, before interpreting any positive. This has not been done.
- Establish whether the survey blocks were treated with formic acid during processing, which Wadsworth 2011 notes reduces prion infectivity by about two to three orders of magnitude and would make a negative result uninterpretable.
