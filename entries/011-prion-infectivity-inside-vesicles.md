## Claim as stated

The claim circulating in the literature and in review articles is that prions travel inside extracellular vesicles — commonly called exosomes — and that this is a route of spread between cells and between hosts.

This entry examines the step underneath that claim, which is rarely stated and has not been established: **that the infectious prion material in these preparations is enclosed within vesicles, rather than free aggregate that happens to pellet alongside them.**

It is the assumption [[001-the-exosome-prion-chain|Entry 001]] rests on. Entry 001 traces the transmission evidence and finds it holds for the pellet; this entry asks what is actually in the pellet.

## Evidence base

Preparations described as exosomal are produced by spinning culture medium at roughly 100,000 × g. Prion protein in its misfolded form aggregates, and those aggregates sediment in the same range. So the standard preparation is a mixture by construction, and the label names only one of its components.

## Untested assumption

Three things sit under the claim, none of them resolved.

1. **Co-sedimentation is not enclosure.** Free prion aggregate and small vesicles pellet together. A method that separates by density rather than speed is needed before the fraction can be called vesicular, and the research notes record at least one paper introducing a sucrose cushion explicitly in order to separate the two.
2. **The usual localisation test cannot work here.** Enclosure inside a vesicle is normally shown by protease protection: material inside is shielded, material outside is digested. Misfolded prion protein is defined by its resistance to protease. The assay therefore returns the same answer whether the protein is inside a vesicle or free in solution. This is a structural limitation of the method against this analyte, not a lapse by any particular study.
3. **The better-separated comparison points the other way.** Where large and small vesicles have been compared with the isolation and characterisation controls reported, the research notes record the large fraction carrying substantially more seeding activity and infecting cells more efficiently — that is, the active material sits in the fraction the field did not name "exosomes".

## Evidence tiers

What T1–T4 mean for this claim.

- **T1** Prion protein is **detected** in a preparation described as vesicular.
- **T2** It is shown to be **inside** vesicles by a test that does not depend on protease resistance.
- **T3** The **infectivity**, not merely the protein, is shown to travel with the enclosed fraction after density separation.
- **T4** Enclosed infectivity is **quantified against** the free-aggregate fraction from the same preparation, in an animal.

The claim is a T3/T4 claim. The evidence currently reaches **T1**.

### The large-versus-small vesicle comparison
Verification: Identity unresolved
Tags: Not a primary test

Recorded in the research notes as verified directly, with isolation speeds, marker panels and cryo-electron-microscopy sizes reported: large vesicles carrying roughly 20–26 times more prion seeding activity than small ones, and infecting cells between about 1.2 and 24.6 times better.

- **What it shows, if the citation resolves:** the seeding-active material is concentrated in the fraction that is not the one called exosomes, which inverts the usual framing.
- **What it does not show:** whether either fraction's activity is enclosed rather than adherent, and seeding activity is not infectivity.
- **Files:** none; see above.

## Where it stands

- **Established.** Preparations called exosomal contain prion protein, and material from them transmits disease on intracerebral injection into PrP-overexpressing mice (see [[001-the-exosome-prion-chain|Entry 001]], where that chain is verified at source).
- **Unresolved.** Whether the transmitting material is inside vesicles. The preparation is a mixture by construction, and the standard enclosure assay cannot distinguish the cases for this protein.
- **Pointing away from the claim.** The better-separated comparison recorded in the research notes puts the activity in the fraction the field named least carefully.

## What would settle it

- **Antibody capture on a vesicle surface marker** (a tetraspanin), then assay the captured population for prion seeding and infectivity. If the claim holds, captured vesicles carry the activity; if it does not, the activity stays in the uncaptured material.
- **Cryo-electron microscopy of individual labelled vesicles**, which images enclosure directly rather than inferring it.
- **Not** a better-run protease-protection experiment. The assay cannot answer this question for this analyte at any level of care, because the readout and the property being tested are the same thing.

> [!summary]
> Prion protein present in vesicle preparations → established.
> Prion protein inside the vesicles → not demonstrated; the standard test cannot show it.
> Vesicle-enclosed material as a route of spread → untested in an animal.
