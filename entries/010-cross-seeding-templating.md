## Claim as stated

The strong form of the claim is that misfolded protein A acts as a structural mould and protein B copies its fold — the same templating mechanism a prion uses on copies of itself, applied across two different proteins. This is what "cross-seeding" means in the prion sense.

Four distinct things are reported under the one word "cross-seeding," and only the first is templating:

1. **True templating** — A's aggregate is a mould and B adopts its fold.
2. **Surface catalysis** — A's fibril surface is a generic site where B nucleates, a sticky scaffold rather than a specific mould.
3. **Co-aggregation** — A and B build a mixed fibril together rather than one converting the other.
4. **A permissive environment** — A's presence damages cells in a way that makes B aggregate, with no physical contact between the two proteins.

All four predict that two pathologies travel together. Most published experiments cannot tell them apart, and the distinction is what the claim turns on.

## Evidence base

Cross-seeding is easy to demonstrate in a test tube, patchier in animals, and in humans almost entirely inferred from co-occurring pathologies. The most-studied pair is amyloid-beta (Aβ) and tau; others are alpha-synuclein with tau, Aβ with alpha-synuclein, Aβ with the prion protein PrP, and TDP-43 with tau.

**The concentration gap.** Aβ in normal brain is in the picomolar range, and CSF Aβ42 is reported at "~0.75 ng/ml" (Fagiani et al. 2021). The concentration above which Aβ42 aggregates on its own — its critical aggregation concentration — was measured at "about 90 nM" (Novo et al. 2018). Test-tube cross-seeding is commonly run at 100 nM to 10 µM: for example, Vasconcelos et al. 2016 incubated tau monomer at 66.7 µM with Aβ seeds at 6.67 µM. That is thousands of times the measured CSF concentration, and is the main reason test-tube results are read as proof of possibility rather than proof of relevance. Defenders note that local concentrations near a plaque may be far higher than bulk measurements, which is plausible and largely unmeasured.

## Untested assumption

That the co-occurrence of two pathologies, and their acceleration of one another, is produced by direct templating rather than by one of the other three routes. A separate assumption is that an interaction shown at micromolar concentrations in a tube reflects what happens at the picomolar-to-nanomolar concentrations in a brain. Neither is established for any protein pair in human disease.

A further confound is the base rate: one review notes that "91%" of people over 80 have more than one neuropathology at autopsy (notes, §11, citing a 2026 review), so co-occurrence is close to the expected background, and shared upstream causes — ageing, failing protein clearance, inflammation — would produce the same pattern with no cross-seeding at all.

## Evidence tiers

What T1–T4 mean for this claim.

- **T1** Two pathologies are **observed together** in human tissue, or one is associated with more of the other.
- **T2** Cross-seeding is **shown under model conditions**: in a cell-free assay or cultured cells, A's aggregate accelerates B's.
- **T3** Cross-seeding is **shown in an animal**, by injecting A and recovering B pathology.
- **T4** **Direct templating** is established as the mechanism in the real system — distinguished from surface catalysis, co-aggregation and a permissive environment, at physiological concentrations.

The claim above is a **T4** claim. The evidence reaches **T2 and T3**, and at T3 the mechanism is disputed. No T4 evidence exists for any pair in human disease.

## What would settle it

- **If the claim holds:** injecting purified A into an animal that does not overexpress B, at physiological levels, yields B pathology templated on A's conformation, with the two proteins in physical contact and the effect absent when A is removed. An atomic structure of a mixed A–B fibril from human brain would show the two folds interleaved.
- **If it doesn't:** B pathology would track a permissive environment (plaque-associated cell stress, inflammation) rather than contact with A, and would appear where A is absent but the environment is present.
- **The discriminating test** is to separate templating from the three indirect routes in the same system, at brain concentrations — which most published designs do not do.

## Current status

Mixed, and it differs by protein pair.

- **Aβ and tau.** That amyloid pathology accelerates tau pathology is well established. That it does so by templating is contested: the two most carefully controlled mouse studies (He et al. 2018; Bennett et al. 2017) concluded against direct templating, favouring a permissive environment. The main positive cell-free result (Vasconcelos et al. 2016) stands but has not displaced them.
- **Alpha-synuclein and tau.** The best evidence for genuine cross-seeding: purified synuclein fibrils induce tau pathology in mice, and the effect is strain-dependent (Guo et al. 2013). Williams et al. 2020 report it runs in both directions in these models.
- **Aβ and PrP.** Cross-seeding in vitro and mutual acceleration in mice (Morales et al. 2010), but no report of Aβ converting normal PrP into the infectious PrPSc form. The most direct test of the prion-to-Aβ direction was negative (Rasmussen et al. 2018, from its title and abstract), and the best-characterised link turned out to be cell signalling, not templating (Ezpeleta et al. 2019).
- A direct physical interaction between TDP-43 and tau is reported, but the one study to measure its effect on seeding found inhibition, not promotion (notes, §11, citing Simonetti et al. 2025); not traced here.

### 2010 — Aβ and PrP: cross-seeding in vitro, acceleration in mice
Tier: T3
Verification: Verified at source
Tags: Peer-reviewed

Morales R, Estrada LD, Diaz-Espinoza R, et al. "Molecular cross talk between misfolded proteins in animal models of Alzheimer's and prion diseases." *Journal of Neuroscience* 2010;30(13):4528–4535. [doi:10.1523/jneurosci.5924-09.2010](https://doi.org/10.1523/jneurosci.5924-09.2010) · PMC2859074

> "in vitro experiments showed that protein misfolding can be enhanced by a cross-seeding mechanism."

- **What it shows:** Prion-infected Alzheimer-model mice had ">10-fold greater plaque area" than uninfected ones, and in vitro PrPSc accelerated Aβ aggregation and Aβ fibrils accelerated PrP misfolding. The strongest positive claim for an Aβ–PrP molecular interaction.
- **What it does not show:** Whether the acceleration is templating. The authors list three possible explanations, including impaired clearance and cell stress, alongside direct interaction. No conversion of normal PrP to the infectious form is reported.

### 2013 — Alpha-synuclein templates tau, strain-dependently
Tier: T3
Verification: Verified at source

Guo JL, Covell DJ, Daniels JP, et al. "Distinct α-synuclein strains differentially promote tau inclusions in neurons." *Cell* 2013;154(1):103–117. [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2013.05.057](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2013.05.057) · PMC3820001

> "we discovered two distinct strains of synthetic α-synuclein fibrils that demonstrated striking differences in the efficiency of cross-seeding tau aggregation, both in neuron cultures and in vivo."

- **What it shows:** Pre-formed α-synuclein fibrils cross-seed tau in neurons and mice, and the effect depends on the α-synuclein strain — the same strain logic that governs mammalian prions, applied across two proteins. The best case for genuine heterotypic templating.
- **What it does not show:** Templating at physiological concentrations in human disease; the fibrils are synthetic and administered.

### 2017 — Aβ enhances tau seeding, attributed to an indirect route
Tier: T3
Verification: Verified at source

Bennett RE, DeVos SL, Dujardin S, et al. "Enhanced Tau Aggregation in the Presence of Amyloid β." *American Journal of Pathology* 2017;187(7):1601–1612. [doi:10.1016/j.ajpath.2017.03.011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajpath.2017.03.011) · PMC5500829

> "these data provide evidence that amyloid β acts to enhance tau pathology by increasing the formation of tau species capable of seeding new aggregates."

- **What it shows:** Tau from human brains with plaques had "an approximately twofold to threefold enhancement of tau seeding" over tau from plaque-free brains at the same Braak stage; APP/PS1-rTg4510 mice had "a threefold increase in tau seeding activity." - **What it does not show:** Direct templating. The authors propose Aβ acts through intermediate factors such as inflammation, not by moulding tau directly.

### 2020 — Tau and alpha-synuclein cross-seed in both directions
Tier: T3
Verification: Verified at source
Tags: Peer-reviewed

Williams T, Sorrentino Z, Weinrich M, et al. "Differential cross-seeding properties of tau and α-synuclein in mouse models of tauopathy and synucleinopathy." *Brain Communications* 2020;2(2):fcaa090. [doi:10.1093/braincomms/fcaa090](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcaa090) · PMC7567170

> "human α-synuclein fibrils can efficiently cross-seed human tau … human tau fibrils can also induce human α-synuclein pathogenesis."

- **What it shows:** In a tauopathy model, α-synuclein fibrils induced tau pathology; in a synucleinopathy model, tau fibrils induced α-synuclein pathology. Cross-seeding runs in both directions, with the efficiency depending on the model.

## Where it stands

- **Established.** Amyloid proteins can accelerate one another's aggregation in vitro, and co-pathologies are common in human brains. Strain-dependent cross-seeding of tau by α-synuclein is shown in mice.
- **Still open.** Whether this proceeds by direct templating or by surface catalysis, co-aggregation or a permissive environment — and whether any of it occurs at brain concentrations. For the most-studied pair, Aβ and tau, the best-controlled animal studies point away from direct templating.
    - inject purified A into an animal not overexpressing B, at physiological levels, and test for templated B pathology in contact with A
    - an atomic structure of a mixed A–B fibril from human brain
    - a design that separates templating from the three indirect routes in one system

> [!summary]
> Chemical cross-seeding (in vitro) → well supported.
> Direct templating as the mechanism → unproven, and contested for Aβ–tau.
> At physiological brain concentrations → not demonstrated for any pair.
